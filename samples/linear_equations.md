# Linear Equations in One Variable

**Grade:** 8
**Subject:** Mathematics

---

## Introduction

Mathematics is often used to solve problems involving unknown quantities. A linear equation in one variable is one of the simplest mathematical models used to represent such situations.

---

## Definition

A **linear equation in one variable** is an equation that contains only one variable raised to the first power.

### General Form

$$ax + b = c$$

where:
- $a$, $b$, and $c$ are constants
- $a \neq 0$
- $x$ is the unknown variable

### Examples

- $x + 7 = 15$
- $3x = 18$
- $4x - 5 = 19$
- $5x + 8 = 3x + 20$

---

## Properties

The same mathematical operation performed on one side of an equation must also be performed on the other side.

Equality remains unchanged when:

- the same number is **added** to both sides
- the same number is **subtracted** from both sides
- both sides are **multiplied** by the same non-zero number
- both sides are **divided** by the same non-zero number

---

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

**Solve:** $x + 9 = 21$

Subtract 9 from both sides:

$$x = 12$$

---

### Example 2

**Solve:** $3x + 5 = 20$

Subtract 5 from both sides:

$$3x = 15$$

Divide both sides by 3:

$$x = 5$$

---

### Example 3

**Solve:** $5x - 8 = 27$

Add 8 to both sides:

$$5x = 35$$

Divide both sides by 5:

$$x = 7$$

---

## Word Problem

Riya bought some notebooks costing ₹40 each and spent a total of ₹280.

Let the number of notebooks be $x$.

$$40x = 280$$

$$x = 7$$

---

## Applications

Linear equations are widely used in:

- Shopping calculations
- Business and finance
- Budget planning
- Age problems
- Distance and speed calculations
- Science experiments
- Engineering calculations

---

## Learning Objectives

Students should be able to:

1. Define a linear equation.
2. Solve simple linear equations.
3. Solve equations involving variables on both sides.
4. Translate word problems into equations.
5. Verify the obtained solution.

## Keywords

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Equation | A mathematical statement asserting equality of two expressions |
| Variable | An unknown quantity, typically represented by a letter |
| Constant | A fixed numerical value |
| Coefficient | The numerical factor of a variable term |
| Solution | The value of the variable that satisfies the equation |
| Equality | The condition of being equal on both sides |
| Transpose | Moving a term from one side of an equation to the other |

---

## Common Student Misconceptions

> ⚠️ Watch out for these frequent errors:

- Changing the sign **incorrectly** while moving terms across the equation
- Dividing **only one side** of the equation instead of both
- Forgetting to perform the **same operation on both sides**
- Assuming every equation has **more than one solution**
- Treating **coefficients and variables** separately

---

## Summary

Linear equations provide a systematic method for solving unknown quantities. They form the foundation for algebra and are extensively used in higher mathematics, economics, engineering, and data science.
